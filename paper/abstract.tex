Replay models guide the selection of elastic LLM-serving policies, but accurate
request timing and aggregate resource estimates need not preserve deployment
decisions. We present FidelityLoop, a closed-loop validation framework that
tests policy feasibility and resource use across replay and physical execution.
A shared policy interface and identity ledger align requests, lifecycle
transitions, and control actions across replay, vLLM, and SGLang, with Vidur
as an external service model. Measurements on two A100 GPUs and replay
contrasts reveal two failure mechanisms: startup timing can cross a controller's
cooldown boundary and alter subsequent actions, while resource errors can
cancel in aggregate despite temporal misalignment. Tests with rule-based and
replay-trained PPO policies show that a startup proxy can produce false
rejections in one deployment context and false acceptances in another.
These errors change policy selection: retrospective screening selects a
candidate that fails all six physical repeats; in another tested pool, it
selects a feasible candidate costing 58.95\% more than the lowest-cost feasible
alternative under the scenario ledger. FidelityLoop connects these decision
errors to execution traces and makes the validation process reusable. We
release paired traces from 197 physical runs, CPU analysis tools, and a
fixed-input Vidur replay package under an explicit resource and synthetic-API
cost contract.
