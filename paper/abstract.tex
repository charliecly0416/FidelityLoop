Elastic LLM-serving policies change the capacity on which their own queues
and control decisions depend. Accurate request-time predictions therefore do
not establish whether a policy will meet deadlines after deployment. We
present FidelityLoop, a closed-loop validation framework that runs rules,
a deadline guard, and replay-trained PPO policies through a shared interface.
Request identities align service timing, resource trajectories, control actions,
and deadline-aware costs across replay, vLLM, and SGLang execution.
In 36 preregistered runs on two A100 GPUs, a calibrated replay model improves
matched-request timing while worsening occupied-resource prediction.
Startup--cooldown interactions explain altered actions; retrospective replay with a Vidur
service backend also exposes timing errors hidden by aggregate agreement.
Tests on frozen PPO checkpoints, additional arrivals, and a 14B port show
that a startup proxy that detects failed policies in one context can reject
feasible policies or accept failing ones in another. These errors have practical
consequences: retrospective replay selection chooses a policy that fails all
six physical repeats; in another tested pool, it selects a feasible policy
costing 58.95\% more than the lowest-cost feasible alternative under the
scenario ledger. FidelityLoop makes both policies and their evaluators
testable against physical deadline and resource outcomes. We release paired traces from 197 physical runs
and CPU analysis tools under an explicit resource and synthetic-API cost contract.
