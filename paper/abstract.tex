Elastic LLM-serving policies depend on when capacity becomes available, not just
on request service times. FidelityLoop evaluates these policies by connecting
replay predictions to physical lifecycle execution and deadline-aware resource
accounting. We ask whether a policy selected in replay still meets its deadlines
when deployed. In a preregistered study of
36 Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct runs on two A100 GPUs, service calibration improves
matched-request timing while worsening occupied-resource prediction; trajectory
analysis explains why aggregate error is insufficient for closed-loop evaluation.
We then execute five frozen PPO checkpoints and an all2 capacity anchor in a
continuous 32-run campaign on the same registered serving stack, using a
separately re-provisioned runtime lock. Twenty runs meet every online and offline
deadline: three checkpoints pass all six runs and two fail all six. Both training
environments produce passing and failing checkpoints. The service-calibrated
replay with the original startup endpoint (C) predicts feasibility correctly on
32/32 runs, with 0.0804 scenario-USD cost MAE. Changing startup to 30 seconds
(C30) improves the earlier resource metric but yields only 20/32 correct
feasibility predictions and 0.4657 cost MAE. These frozen predictions show why
policy evaluation must check deadline feasibility alongside resource accuracy.
The artifact retains every attempt and negative result under the studied
workloads, hardware, and synthetic-API cost assumptions.
