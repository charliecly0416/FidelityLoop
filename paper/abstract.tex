Elastic LLM-serving policies depend on when capacity becomes available, not just
on request service times. We present and implement FidelityLoop, a closed-loop
validation framework connecting replay predictions, physical lifecycle execution,
and deadline-aware resource accounting. In a preregistered study of 36
Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct runs on two A100 GPUs, the calibrated replay improves
matched-request timing while worsening occupied-resource prediction; trajectory
analysis explains why aggregate error is insufficient for closed-loop evaluation.
To test whether replay-selected policies remain feasible when deployed, we train
PPO scheduling policies in the framework's replay environment under two startup-time
assumptions. We select and freeze five checkpoints and evaluate them alongside an always-on
two-GPU reference in a continuous 32-run campaign on the same registered serving
stack, using a separately re-provisioned runtime lock. Three checkpoints meet
every online and offline deadline in all six runs; two fail all six. Both training
environments produce passing and failing checkpoints. Replay with calibrated
service times and the historical startup proxy (C) predicts feasibility correctly
on 32/32 runs, with 0.0804 scenario-USD cost MAE. Changing startup to 30 seconds
(C30) improves the earlier resource metric but yields only 20/32 correct
feasibility predictions and 0.4657 cost MAE. Thus lower resource prediction error
does not guarantee better deployment-feasibility prediction. The study preserves
all attempts and negative results under its workload, hardware, and synthetic-API
cost assumptions.
